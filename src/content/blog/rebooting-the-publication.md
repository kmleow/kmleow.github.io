For several years, this publication sat largely dormant. 

It was not for a lack of ideas or engineering problems to discuss. Over the past two decades of working across software engineering, systems architecture, and engineering leadership, the backlog of architectural war stories, production lessons, and technical observations only continued to expand. 

The honest reason I stepped away from active blogging was far simpler: **traditional long-form technical writing is an immense time sink**. 

When you are actively architecting scalable platforms, steering engineering transformation, and solving complex production bottlenecks, spending ten to fifteen hours manually drafting, polishing, formatting, and proofreading a single comprehensive article often feels impossible to justify. The friction between having a high-value architectural thesis in your head and turning it into an exhaustive essay was simply too high.

Today, that calculus has fundamentally shifted. 

---

## The AI-Augmented Authorship Model

The emergence of capable LLMs and agentic coding harnesses has transformed how I approach technical expression. 

To be clear: **AI does not originate architectural opinions**. It cannot experience a brutal cascading failover during a migration, it cannot feel the operational cost of premature microservice decoupling, and it cannot invent genuine engineering battle scars. 

The ideation, technical stances, trade-off analyses, and architectural judgments across this publication remain **100% human—originating directly from myself**.

```
[ Human Experience & Battle Scars ] ──► [ Core Architectural Thesis ]
                                                │
                                                ▼
[ Publication: High-Signal Insights ] ◄── [ AI Synthesis & Drafting Harness ]
```

What AI solves is the drafting bottleneck. It serves as a tireless execution harness—taking my structural outlines, technical diagrams, and engineering stances, and rapidly synthesising them into clean, polished prose. What previously required multiple weekends of mechanical drafting can now be composed and refined in a fraction of the time, allowing me to share practical insights without sacrificing operational focus.

---

## The Editorial Purge: Aggressive Signal-to-Noise Ratio

As part of rebooting this site, I undertook an aggressive editorial cleanup. 

Over the years, the site had accumulated over 50 articles. A candid audit revealed that the vast majority had outlived their usefulness:
* **Outdated Tutorials**: Transient configuration guides for software versions and desktop utilities that no longer exist.
* **Low-Value Snippets**: Minor workarounds that modern platforms now handle natively out of the box.
* **Legacy Sponsorships**: Posts that added zero architectural value for senior engineering leaders.

I purged 49 articles outright. 

I retained only six flagship technical deep-dives—covering Smart Queue Management (SQM) for bufferbloat, Linux kernel network fastpaths, Zero Trust mTLS ingress filtering, policy-based multi-tunnel routing, ephemeral CI/CD runners, and expressive functional code craftsmanship. 

Each of these remaining pillars was thoroughly rewritten and polished. The objective was to strip away novice tutorial fluff, elevate the engineering stakes, and ensure every remaining piece delivers immediate architectural signal to engineering leaders and software architects.

## Preserving Minimalist Engineering

In redesigning the publication, the goal was to keep the architecture clean, fast, and disciplined. 

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      kmleow.com                        │
│   Astro 5 Static Build  •  Tailwind CSS  •  No Bloat   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                           │
         ┌─────────────────┴─────────────────┐
         ▼                                   ▼
[ Clean Architecture ]              [ LinkedIn Channels ]
• Zero third-party trackers         • Peer engineering dialogue
• No intrusive comment bloat        • High-context discussion
```

I rebuilt the publication on Astro 5 and Tailwind CSS. The stack is straightforward, predictable, and maintainable. 

Crucially, I eliminated third-party tracking bloat:
* **No Disqus or Heavy Comment Widgets**: Third-party comment plugins add megabytes of JavaScript, inject third-party ad networks, and invite spam. 
* **Conversations on LinkedIn**: High-signal engineering discussions belong where professionals actually collaborate. By driving discussion directly to LinkedIn, I preserve the speed of the site while engaging directly with fellow architects and engineering directors.

## What to Expect: The Road Ahead

Returning to writing is not about chasing pageviews or posting trivial tutorials. The focus from here on is documenting the real-world complexities of modern engineering leadership and transformation. 

Here are the four key themes I intend to explore:

### 1. AI-First Engineering & Autonomous SDLC
Moving beyond simple autocomplete towards orchestrating autonomous development pipelines, agentic testing harnesses, and multi-agent code workflows. How technology leaders can reshape software delivery to amplify engineering leverage safely and pragmatically.

### 2. Engineering Craftsmanship & Mentorship
Cultivating engineering discipline across growing teams. Designing readable, composable codebases that reduce cognitive and operational friction, while mentoring technical leads to build with ownership, rigor, and long-term maintainability.

### 3. Systems Decoupling & Microservices
Guiding complex engineering transformations from monolithic architectures to resilient, event-driven services. Applying Domain-Driven Design (DDD) to establish clear service boundaries, avoid the trap of premature distributed systems, and execute migrations without breaking operational continuity.

### 4. Cloud Architecture & Operational Resilience
Prioritising boring, battle-tested technologies over short-lived industry hype. Designing high-availability hybrid cloud platforms that prioritise observability, cost predictability, and zero-trust security from day one.
